FINANCIAL SAFETY

Protect your money
before you have to recover it.

Digital payments make life easier — but they also create new ways for criminals to target people. Learn the habits that keep your money, accounts and identity safer.

UPI OTP & PIN Phishing SIM Swap Remote Access Passwords Simulator Emergency

🔐 The golden rule of financial safety

Never share your OTP, UPI PIN, ATM PIN, CVV, password or banking credentials with anyone.

A legitimate bank or payment service should not need you to reveal your secret authentication credentials over a call, message or social-media chat.

01 · DIGITAL PAYMENTS

UPI safety

UPI is designed for convenient digital payments, but the biggest risk is often social engineering — convincing you to perform an action yourself.

📲

Receiving money

Receiving money does not normally require you to enter your UPI PIN.

  • Check the transaction notification.
  • Do not enter your PIN just to receive money.
  • Verify the amount and sender.
🔗

Unknown QR codes

Scanning a QR code can initiate a payment flow. Do not scan random codes because someone says you will “receive” money.

  • Check who requested the payment.
  • Read the payment screen carefully.
  • Never rush because of pressure.
👀

Verify before paying

Before confirming a payment, check the recipient, amount and purpose.

  • Check the displayed name.
  • Check the amount.
  • Stop if anything looks unusual.
02 · AUTHENTICATION

OTP, PIN & CVV

These are security credentials, not information to be casually shared with customer-care agents, friends, delivery personnel or callers.

❌ Never share

OTPs, UPI PINs, ATM PINs, CVVs, passwords, card credentials or authentication codes.

✅ Keep private

Enter sensitive credentials only inside the legitimate banking or payment interface you intentionally opened.

❌ Don't trust urgency

“Your account will be blocked in 5 minutes” is a pressure tactic. Stop and verify independently.

✅ Call independently

If you need support, find the official contact details yourself rather than trusting a number sent by a stranger.

03 · SOCIAL ENGINEERING

Phishing, smishing & vishing

Scammers may use email, SMS, phone calls or social media to create fear, urgency or excitement.

📧

Phishing

Fraudulent emails or websites designed to trick you into revealing information or making a payment.

Red flag: suspicious links, unexpected attachments or urgent requests.

💬

Smishing

Phishing delivered through SMS or messaging platforms.

Red flag: “Click here to update KYC” or “account will be blocked.”

📞

Vishing

Fraud through phone calls where the caller pretends to be from a bank, company or authority.

Red flag: asking for OTP, PIN or immediate payment.

🚨 Remember: caller ID is not proof of identity

A caller may know your name, phone number, bank name or other basic information. That does not prove that they represent the organisation they claim to represent.

04 · MOBILE SECURITY

SIM-swap & mobile-number risks

Your mobile number may be connected to banking, payment and account-recovery systems. Protect it like an important part of your financial identity.

📱

Watch for sudden loss of service

Unexpected loss of mobile connectivity can have multiple causes, but if it happens suddenly and remains unexplained, investigate it.

🔒

Protect your SIM

  • Use a SIM PIN where appropriate.
  • Protect your phone and email accounts.
  • Don't publish unnecessary personal information.
🏦

Act quickly if something feels wrong

If you suspect unauthorised activity, contact your telecom provider and financial institution through official channels.

05 · DEVICE SECURITY

Remote-access app scams

A scammer may ask you to install an application so they can “help” with a refund, KYC, technical problem or account verification.

⚠️ Don't give strangers control of your device

Remote-access software can allow another person to view or interact with your device. Never install such software merely because an unexpected caller tells you to.

❌ “Install this app for verification”

Stop. Verify the request independently through the organisation's official support channel.

❌ “Show me your screen”

Don't share your screen while opening banking or payment applications.

✅ Use official apps

Download financial applications through trusted, official app stores and verify the publisher.

✅ Review app permissions

Remove unnecessary permissions and uninstall apps you no longer need.

06 · ACCOUNT SECURITY

Passwords & multi-factor authentication

A strong password is useful, but account security works best when multiple protective layers are used.

🔑

Use unique passwords

Avoid using the same password across banking, email, social media and other services.

🛡️

Enable MFA

Where available, use multi-factor authentication to add another layer beyond your password.

🚫

Avoid predictable passwords

Names, birthdays, phone numbers and simple patterns are poor choices for important accounts.

Act quickly.

If you notice an unauthorised financial transaction, don't wait for the situation to “sort itself out.” Contact the relevant financial institution through its official channels and report suspected cybercrime promptly.
