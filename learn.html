01

Money Basics

Before investing or taking a loan, understand what money actually does.

💰

Income

Money you receive. It can come from salary, freelance work, business, interest, rent or other sources.

💸

Expenses

Money you spend on needs, wants, bills, education, transport, entertainment and other purposes.

🪙

Savings

Money kept aside instead of being immediately spent. Savings can help handle short-term needs and emergencies.

📈

Investment

Money put into an asset with the expectation that it may generate returns or grow over time.

🔄

Cash Flow

The movement of money into and out of your finances during a period.

🎯

Financial Goal

A specific money objective with an amount and usually a time horizon.

💡 Simple example

If you receive ₹25,000 and spend ₹20,000, your cash flow leaves ₹5,000 before considering other adjustments. The next question is what you do intentionally with that ₹5,000.

02

Budgeting

A budget is a plan for where your money should go before it disappears.

📋

Track first

Record your actual income and spending before deciding where you need to make changes.

🏠

Needs

Expenses that support essential living, such as food, housing, utilities and necessary transport.

🎮

Wants

Non-essential spending that adds enjoyment or convenience.

🆘

Emergency Fund

Money reserved for unexpected expenses such as emergencies, repairs or temporary loss of income.

📆

Short vs Long Term

A phone purchase next month and retirement decades away require very different financial planning.

🔍

Review

A budget is not punishment. Review it regularly and adjust it when income, priorities or expenses change.

03

Saving

Saving creates financial breathing room.

Why save?

  • Handle unexpected expenses.
  • Prepare for planned purchases.
  • Reduce dependence on expensive debt.
  • Build a foundation before investing.

Saving vs Investing

Saving and investing serve different purposes. Savings are generally intended for accessibility and relatively short-term needs, while investments are generally used for longer-term growth and involve different levels of risk.

04

Banking

Understand what your bank account actually does and what different banking products mean.

🏦

Savings Account

A deposit account designed for everyday banking and holding money while generally earning some interest.

🔒

Fixed Deposit

Money deposited for a specified period at a stated rate according to the product's terms.

🔁

Recurring Deposit

A deposit product where money is generally deposited periodically for a specified period.

📱

UPI

A digital payment system that enables bank-account-based transactions through participating applications.

🧾

Bank Statement

A record of account transactions that can help you monitor money coming in and going out.

🔑

Banking Credentials

Passwords, PINs, OTPs and other authentication information should never be casually shared.

05

Credit & Debt

Borrowing can be useful, but understanding the cost of borrowing is essential.

💳

Credit

Access to money or purchasing power now with an obligation to repay according to agreed terms.

📊

Credit Score

A numerical representation generated from credit information according to a particular scoring model.

🧮

Interest

The cost associated with borrowing money, or the return paid on certain deposits or investments.

⚠️

Debt Trap

A situation where repeated borrowing and repayment obligations make it increasingly difficult to meet financial commitments.

⚠️
Understand the total cost.

Don't compare loans only by EMI. Look at interest rate, tenure, processing charges, other fees and the total amount payable.

06

Insurance

Insurance is primarily about managing financial risk from uncertain events.

🏥

Health Insurance

Provides coverage according to policy terms for specified healthcare-related expenses.

👨‍👩‍👧

Life Insurance

Provides financial protection according to the terms of a life-insurance policy.

🚗

General Insurance

Can cover areas such as vehicles, property, travel and other specified risks.

📄

Policy Terms

Always understand exclusions, waiting periods, limits, premiums and claim conditions before purchasing insurance.

07

Taxes

Taxes are an important part of personal financial planning.

Income Tax

Income tax is imposed according to the applicable tax laws and a person's taxable income and circumstances. Tax rules, rates and deductions can change, so current official information should always be checked.

Tax Planning ≠ Tax Evasion

Legitimate tax planning involves understanding applicable provisions and organizing finances within the law. Tax evasion involves unlawful concealment or misrepresentation.

08

Retirement Planning

Retirement planning is about preparing for future financial needs over a long horizon.

⏳

Start Early

A longer investment horizon can allow more time for contributions and potential compounding, although returns are never guaranteed.

📈

Inflation

Inflation means the general level of prices increases over time, which can reduce the purchasing power of money.

🎯

Retirement Goal

Consider future expenses, expected lifestyle, inflation, retirement age and available resources when planning.

09

Digital Money

Digital convenience should never mean ignoring security.

📱

UPI Payments

Check the recipient before authorizing a payment. Understand that entering a UPI PIN generally authorizes a transaction.

🔐

OTP

Treat one-time passwords as sensitive authentication information and do not disclose them to callers or strangers.

🎣

Phishing

Fraudsters may use fake messages, websites or emails to trick people into revealing information or making payments.

📞

Fake Customer Care

Verify support numbers through official websites or applications instead of trusting numbers found in random posts or messages.

10

Advanced Financial Concepts

Once the foundation is strong, these concepts help you understand more complex financial decisions.

📊

Asset Allocation

How money is distributed among different asset classes according to goals, time horizon and risk considerations.

🧩

Diversification

Spreading exposure across investments can reduce concentration in a single asset, although it cannot eliminate investment risk.

🔄

Compounding

Returns can themselves generate returns over time. The effect depends on the rate, time and how frequently returns are compounded.

📉

Volatility

A measure describing how much the value of an asset or market can fluctuate over time.

⚖️

Risk vs Return

Different investments have different risk characteristics. Higher potential return does not mean guaranteed higher return.

🧠

Behavioural Finance

Emotions, habits and cognitive biases can influence financial decisions.

💡

Money Myths vs Reality

Financial misinformation can be expensive. Learn to question absolute claims.

MYTH

“Investing always means buying stocks.”


REALITY

Investments can include many different asset classes and products, each with its own characteristics and risks.

MYTH

“A higher return is automatically better.”


REALITY

Return should be considered together with risk, time horizon, liquidity and your financial objective.

MYTH

“Credit cards are free money.”


REALITY

A credit card provides access to credit. Unpaid balances can attract interest and other charges according to the card's terms.

MYTH

“Guaranteed high returns are normal.”


REALITY

Claims of unusually high or guaranteed returns should be treated carefully and independently verified.

Quick Finance Glossary

The words you'll see again and again while learning about money.

Principal Original amount of money invested or borrowed.
Interest Cost of borrowing or return on certain deposits.
Inflation General increase in prices over time.
Liquidity How easily an asset can be converted into cash.
Risk Possibility that an outcome differs from expectations.
Return Gain or loss generated by an investment.
EMI Equated periodic payment used for certain loans.
Asset A resource with economic value.
