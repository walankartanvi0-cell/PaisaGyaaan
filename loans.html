01

Loan Basics

Let's decode the vocabulary lenders use.

💰

Principal

The original amount of money borrowed, before considering interest and other charges.

📈

Interest

The cost charged for borrowing money, generally expressed using a rate and subject to the loan's terms.

📆

Tenure

The period over which the loan is scheduled to be repaid.

🧮

EMI

Equated Monthly Instalment is a periodic payment used for many loans. Its composition between principal and interest can change over time.

🧾

Processing Fee

A fee that may be charged for processing a loan. The amount and conditions depend on the lender and product.

⚠️

Penalty / Charges

Late-payment, prepayment, foreclosure or other charges may apply depending on the product and applicable terms.

02

Understanding EMI

EMI is a payment amount, not the full cost of a loan.

🧩

Principal Component

Part of the payment reduces the outstanding principal, according to the loan's repayment schedule.

💸

Interest Component

Part of the payment goes toward the interest charged according to the applicable calculation.

⏳

Longer Tenure

A longer tenure can reduce the periodic payment in some loan structures but may increase the total interest paid.

Standard reducing-balance EMI formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)

P = principal, r = periodic interest rate, n = number of payment periods. Actual loan calculations depend on the lender's methodology and terms.

03

Interest & Loan Cost

The headline interest rate is only one part of understanding borrowing cost.

📉

Fixed Rate

An interest rate that remains fixed according to the applicable loan terms for the relevant period.

🔄

Floating Rate

An interest rate that can change based on the relevant benchmark, spread and applicable terms.

🧾

Additional Charges

Processing fees, documentation fees, insurance charges, late-payment charges or other applicable costs can affect the total cost.

⚠️
Don't compare loans only by EMI.

A lower EMI can result from a longer repayment period. Compare the interest rate, tenure, total repayment, fees and other applicable charges.

Common Types of Loans

Different loans are designed for different purposes.

Loan Typical Purpose Secured? Important Things to Understand
Home Loan Purchase / construction of eligible property Usually secured Interest rate, tenure, property-related conditions and total repayment.
Education Loan Eligible education expenses Depends on product and amount Eligibility, moratorium, interest, repayment terms and applicable benefits.
Vehicle Loan Purchase of eligible vehicle Usually secured against the vehicle Interest, tenure, down payment and repayment obligations.

05

Credit Cards

A credit card is a credit facility — not extra income.

💳 Credit Limit

The maximum amount of credit available under the card's terms. Your available credit changes as transactions and payments occur.

📅 Billing Cycle

Transactions during a billing period are included in the statement according to the card issuer's schedule.

🧾 Statement

The statement shows transactions, amount due, minimum amount due and payment information according to the card's terms.

⚠️ Minimum Due

Paying only the minimum amount may leave an outstanding balance that can attract applicable interest and charges.

🎁 Rewards

Cashback, reward points and other benefits can have conditions, exclusions, limits or expiry rules.

🔐 Responsible Use

Keep spending within what you can repay and understand the card's interest and fee structure.

06

Buy Now, Pay Later

Splitting a purchase into payments does not make the purchase financially free.

🛒

What it means

BNPL products can allow eligible consumers to pay later or split purchases according to the product's terms.

🔍

Check the Cost

Look for interest, processing charges, late fees and other applicable costs.

🧠

Watch the Habit

Multiple small instalments can accumulate into a significant monthly repayment obligation.

07

Digital Lending Safety

Fast approval does not mean you should skip verification.

✓
Verify the lender

Check whether the lender and lending arrangement are legitimate and appropriately regulated.

✓
Read the Key Facts

Understand the applicable interest rate, charges and repayment obligations.

✓
Check app permissions

Be cautious about applications requesting unnecessary access to personal information.

✓
Don't pay random “release fees”

Unexpected demands for payments through personal accounts or suspicious links are a major warning sign.

✓
Protect your documents

Don't casually share Aadhaar, PAN, OTP, banking credentials or other sensitive information.

✓
Keep repayment records

Maintain statements, receipts and communication relating to your loan.

08

How a Debt Trap Can Develop

Debt becomes dangerous when new borrowing repeatedly becomes necessary to manage old obligations.

1

Unplanned borrowing

A person borrows without fully understanding the repayment cost or affordability.

2

Repayment pressure

EMIs, card balances and other obligations begin consuming a larger portion of available cash flow.

3

New borrowing

Another loan or credit facility is used to meet previous obligations.

🛑
Warning sign

If you regularly need new borrowing to pay existing debt, pause and review your entire debt situation rather than treating each new loan as an isolated decision.

10

Before You Borrow: Checklist

Run through these questions before signing up.

✓
Do I actually need the loan?

Separate a genuine financial need from an impulse purchase.

✓
Can I comfortably repay it?

Consider existing EMIs and regular expenses, not just today's income.

✓
What is the total repayment?

Calculate the full amount payable over the complete tenure.

✓
What are the additional charges?

Check processing, late payment, prepayment and other applicable fees.

✓
What happens if I miss a payment?

Understand consequences before agreeing to the loan.

✓
Have I read the agreement?

Don't accept a financial contract without understanding its important terms.
